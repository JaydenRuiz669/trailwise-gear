THE SCENIC ROUTE · FREE FIELD NOTES

Free stuff I've learned out there.

Not a lecture and not a beginner-only checklist. These are practical lessons from my own adventures, truck maintenance and camp trips. I'm still learning, too.

1. Check the trail before you commit.

Some of the hardest parts of a route can show up near the end. On an Oklahoma mountain trail, the last stretch was the toughest part of the drive. It's taught me not to assume a route stays easy just because the beginning is.

When the terrain changes, stop somewhere safe, look ahead on foot where appropriate, and think about clearance, traction and how you'd turn around. There is no shame in choosing another route.

2. The little maintenance details matter.

Working on my Ford Ranger has taught me that wheel bearings, hardware, clearance and basic checks matter more than chasing the next flashy upgrade. A piece of equipment is only helpful when it fits and is installed correctly.

Before a trail day, check tires, loose parts and any symptoms that could affect safe driving. Don't treat a gear recommendation as a substitute for the manufacturer's instructions or a qualified mechanic's inspection.

3. Simple plans make for better camp nights.

I've camped with a hammock and cooked meals outdoors. What keeps a trip enjoyable isn't buying every gadget; it's thinking through the weather, sleep setup, water, food and the way you'll pack up afterward.

Use the equipment you already know, check local camping and fire restrictions, and make an alternate plan in case conditions change.
